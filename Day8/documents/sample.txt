Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) is an architectural approach that optimizes the output of large language models (LLMs) by referencing an external knowledge base outside of its training data before generating a response.

In a typical RAG system:
1. Document Ingestion: Text documents are loaded and cleaned.
2. Chunking: Large documents are broken down into semantically coherent, manageable pieces called chunks.
3. Embedding Generation: Each chunk is mapped into high-dimensional vector space using an embedding model.
4. Vector Storage: Vector representations and chunk metadata are indexed in a vector store like FAISS.
5. Retrieval: When a user query arrives, it is embedded, and nearest neighbor search retrieves the top-k most relevant chunks.
6. Augmentation & Generation: The retrieved chunks serve as contextual ground truth for an LLM to generate precise, grounded answers with citations.
